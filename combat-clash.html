Interruption & Combat

A Mutual-Risk Clash

Interruption is not a passive defensive reflex. It is an active Kern-versus-Kern clash, drawn from the interrupter's own reserves — the interrupter pushes their own ignited flow directly into contest with the caster's, fighting for control over the same targeted material.

The exchange carries real cost regardless of outcome. A successful interruption breaks the opponent's chain and forces a full re-ignition, but the interrupter has also expended Kern in the process — even victory is a trade, not a free action. A failed attempt is worse: the interrupter has spent resources for no return, typically while physically overcommitted, leaving them exposed to punishment.

Partial interruption is also possible. A rushed or underpowered clash may degrade a technique's output rather than negate it entirely.
