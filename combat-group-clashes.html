Interruption & Combat

Group Clashes

Group clashes are possible, and their effectiveness depends on coordination. An uncoordinated group produces diminishing returns — independent Kern flows interfere with one another as much as they reinforce, so each additional participant contributes less than the last.

A group with genuine trained coordination, however, can approach a near-straight sum, braiding their flows into something close to fully additive force. This distinction gives long-standing, trained teams a real mechanical advantage over ad hoc groups, independent of their individual strength.
