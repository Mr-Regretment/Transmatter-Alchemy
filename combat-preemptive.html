Interruption & Combat

Preemptive Interruption

Disrupting an opponent's ignition before a chain even begins is the highest-value and highest-risk form of interruption, since it denies an entire sequence for a single Kern cost. It is not, however, generally available. Executing it requires anticipating an opponent's intent before it manifests, which in practice requires extensive familiarity with that specific individual — built through prolonged rivalry or training history, not general skill.

The reference point for this mechanic is a well-known moment in Naruto, in which Might Guy explains that years of fighting Kakashi taught him to read his opponent's feet rather than his eyes. It is a relationship-earned capability, not a trainable stat, and is best treated as a narrative device rather than a tracked mechanic.
