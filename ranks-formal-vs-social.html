Ranks & Society

Formal Rank vs. Social Rank

These two tracks are administratively distinct. Formal rank is earned through diplomas, written and practical examinations, and graded field training — the legitimate measure of a practitioner's actual capability. Social rank is conferred through ceremony, a public acknowledgment carrying considerable weight in status and family standing, but no functional authority of its own.

Because these two tracks are separate, they can fall out of alignment. A practitioner may pass every requirement for advancement and simply not yet have undergone the ceremony marking it, remaining addressed by their prior rank socially despite being functionally qualified. Conversely, a ceremonial advancement may be granted early for political or family reasons, ahead of the formal qualifications that would typically justify it.
