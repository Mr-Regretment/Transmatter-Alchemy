Interruption & Combat

Targeting Living Matter

Since T-M operates on electrons, protons, and neutrons without exception, a living body is not exempt in principle — but targeting one is uniquely difficult for reasons that fall directly out of the system's existing rules, rather than requiring any additional in-universe protection.

Focus is nearly impossible to hold

T-M requires focusing on a specific, targetable quantity of material. A living body is the opposite of that: constantly circulating fluid, constantly dividing cells, in motion, never homogeneous. Isolating a precise component within it is an order of magnitude harder than isolating the iron in a motionless blade.

A living body resists preparation

Materials normally need to be prepared before they're easy to manipulate. A living body actively undoes that kind of preparation on its own — it is a self-correcting system. Remove a small quantity of a component and the body immediately begins compensating and redistributing to repair the disruption, in real time, without any conscious effort. A practitioner isn't just fighting the material; they're fighting a system actively working against the intrusion.

Every living person has ambient Kern

Kern is stored in a biological structure, the Cell, and every living person has one — trained or not. This means any attempt to manipulate a living body's matter runs directly into the target's own passive Kern already occupying that space, triggering something functionally identical to an interruption clash, without either party needing to train for it. It is the biological equivalent of an immune response.

This produces a gradient rather than a hard wall: an untrained person has weak ambient Kern, making them difficult but not impossible to target, with any attempt costing the attacker a real clash. A trained practitioner's larger Cell makes passive resistance alone a serious deterrent. A clash between two high-rank practitioners' bodies would be a genuine contest of reserves rather than a clean, one-sided technique.

In most T-M societies, this makes direct body-targeting a rare, feared, and likely heavily restricted specialization — something closer to a banned weapon class than an ordinary technique, reserved culturally (if it exists at all) for the most advanced and most controversial practitioners.
