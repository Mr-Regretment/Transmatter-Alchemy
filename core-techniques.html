Core Mechanics

Techniques: Pattern-Based and Chainable

Techniques are not memorized as individual, fixed recipes. They function as patterns — a technique for breaking a peroxide bond, for instance, generalizes across a range of chemically similar reactions, which keeps the total body of knowledge a practitioner needs to hold in their head at a manageable size.

Techniques can also be chained, with one reaction triggering directly into the next, allowing a skilled practitioner to execute in seconds a sequence that would otherwise take considerable time to perform manually.

Execution quality scales with rank. A Primary attempting a combustion technique may not produce fire at all — the attempt may simply complete the full, unremarkable version of the underlying reaction instead, yielding gas and water rather than flame. The technique does not fail outright so much as resolve into its least impressive outcome.

Looping a chain

It is possible to feed a chain's final Byproduct back into its own first step, effectively looping the sequence. This is not a free energy cycle, however: each loop's Byproduct is made up of less material than what went into that step, and the loop still costs Kern to run. Run it enough times and the usable material shrinks toward nothing, at which point the loop simply stops — a practitioner cannot sustain a chain indefinitely this way, only stretch a given quantity of material further than a single pass would.
