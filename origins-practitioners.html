Origins of Practitioners

Several distinct paths lead to advancement within this system, and a given setting need not restrict itself to only one.

The most common route is sustained study and training — the standard path of classroom instruction, practice, and formal advancement. Some practitioners are born with unusually high natural reserves, independent of lineage. Others inherit elevated capacity through generational compounding. A rarer and more dramatic path involves an altered origin — a childhood accident, experiment, or exposure that artificially inflates Kern reserves well beyond what training or heredity alone would produce, occasionally by a substantial multiple.

Regardless of origin, Kern functions identically once acquired. Altered-origin Kern is entirely natural in its behavior — it ignites, redirects, and clashes exactly as trained or inherited Kern does, with no inherent instability or mechanical disadvantage. The only distinguishing factor is the circumstances of its acquisition, which is left as a narrative rather than a mechanical consideration.
