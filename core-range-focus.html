Core Mechanics

Range and Focus

T-M is not a ranged discipline. A practitioner must focus directly on the specific quantity of material they intend to manipulate — the peroxide in a cup, the powder in a pouch, the iron in a blade — and the technique acts on that focused material, along with whatever nearby matter it draws components from. The practice has more in common with elemental bending than with long-range spellcasting.

Focus itself functions as a resource distinct from Kern. Maintaining an active technique while tracking an opponent and defending against interruption places real demands on a practitioner's attention, and many close engagements are decided by who manages that split more effectively — not by who holds more Kern in reserve.
