The Foundational Split

Everything in this system is built on one distinction.

Electron manipulation is chemistry — bond-breaking, oxidation, converting one compound into another. It is comparatively inexpensive and low-risk, and it's where every practitioner starts. The standard early example is converting hydrogen peroxide into water.

Proton and neutron manipulation is a different category of difficulty entirely. This is not chemistry; it is transmutation — changing what element a material fundamentally is, which requires overcoming the strong nuclear force. The cost is not marginally higher, it is several orders of magnitude greater, and failure is not gentle. A destabilized nucleus can leave behind hazardous byproduct, including radioactive material. Nucleon-level work is therefore restricted to advanced practitioners and treated with the caution of a controlled detonation.
