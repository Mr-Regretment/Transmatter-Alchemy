Ignition and Kern Redirection

Initiating Kern flow is not instantaneous. A practitioner must ignite the flow first — a process with a flat Kern cost and a brief window of vulnerability, comparable to starting an engine. This window is precisely when a technique is most susceptible to disruption.

Once flow is established, however, a practitioner can redirect the residual momentum from a completed reaction directly into the next reaction in a chain, bypassing the ignition cost for every subsequent link. This is the mechanical basis for chaining: a five-step sequence costs a single ignition plus five reaction costs, rather than five full ignitions. The mechanic rewards fluency, since maintaining a clean handoff between reactions requires genuine mastery of the underlying pattern.

If a chain is broken mid-sequence, the redirected flow disperses, and continuing requires paying the full ignition cost again.
